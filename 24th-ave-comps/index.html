04 · Better and Best
04For Better and Best

Three inspections up front.

If we sell on the open market, I want three inspections done before the house lists: home, HVAC, and pest. The reports go in the disclosures, so buyers know the condition before they write and there's less to renegotiate in escrow. An investor buys as-is, so Good doesn't need them.

Home inspection

Roof, raised foundation, electrical, plumbing, and windows. On a 1936 house this report answers the questions every buyer is going to ask anyway.

HVAC inspection

The system was new in 2018, so it's eight years old. The inspector will tell us what it has left. HVAC is the first thing buyers at this price use to renegotiate.

Pest inspection

The house closed with a cleared pest report in 2018. A new report tells us if anything has changed. Most buyers here use FHA, and the appraiser will flag dry rot or damage, so we want to know first.

It costs some money up front and saves a renegotiation later.

05 · Open items
05Open items

Three things that move the number.

  • Condition since 2018. These numbers start from what the house had when you bought it and assume no major repairs. The inspections tell us for sure. If they find something big, we either fix it before we list or price it in. How the floors, paint, kitchen, and bath have held up decides how much of the Best option needs doing.
  • Anything you've changed. If you've added, upgraded, or repaired anything since you bought it, tell me. Upgrades move the number up. Permits matter for anything structural.
  • The competition. 3611 23rd is asking $340,000 with a 3.5% assumable loan. If it's still on the market when we list, it pulls buyers away from the as-is price. If it sells first, that helps us.

02 · The decision
02Occupied or vacant

You have two real options.

The tenants are the biggest variable in your number. Not because buyers dislike tenants, but because an occupied home is harder to sell well.

Here are both paths with honest numbers on each.

Option 1
Sell with the tenants in place
$509,000
List price · likely sale $500,000 to $520,000
  • Showings need notice and tenant cooperation
  • Fewer appointment windows, so fewer buyers through the door
  • Tenant furniture and belongings in the listing photos
  • Less control over how clean the home shows
  • Buyers spend less time inside and move on faster
  • Some buyers hesitate over when they actually get possession
Option 2 · My recommendation
Wait until it is vacant and market ready
$535,000
List price · likely sale $525,000 to $545,000
  • Vacant, so buyers can walk it on their schedule
  • Deep clean and touch-up paint where needed
  • Yard cleaned up, belongings out
  • Professional photography of empty, clean rooms
  • Lockbox access, no appointment friction

This does not mean remodeling.

The $535,000 number assumes clean, empty, and photographed well. It does not assume a new kitchen or new baths. I am not asking you to spend $40,000 to chase $25,000.

The difference between the two paths is roughly $20,000 to $30,000 in gross sale price. That is the number to weigh, and it is not free money. Against it you have to count the mortgage payments, taxes, insurance and utilities you keep paying while the home sits empty, the rent you stop collecting, what the clean-up costs, and how long it actually takes to get the tenants out.

If getting vacancy takes two months and your carrying costs plus lost rent run $3,500 a month, you have spent $7,000 to gain $20,000 to $30,000. That math works. If it takes six months and the tenants fight it, the math gets thin. You know your lease and your tenants better than I do, so run those numbers and tell me what you find.

One middle path exists. If the tenants cooperate well, the home is genuinely clean, and I can get real photography and easy showing access, we can test a little above $509,000 while occupied. I would not count on it, but I will not leave it on the table either.

03 · Your competition
03On your street right now

There is a house for sale on Landis Circle.

529 Landis Circle is listed at $470,000 and has been sitting 44 days. It is 1,595 square feet on an 8,825 square foot lot, so on paper it looks a lot like yours. Every buyer who comes to see your home will find it.

It is not your value, and here is why. It was built in 1938, it has no garage, and the seller says plainly in the listing that it needs repairs they cannot afford. The roof was replaced about three years ago and it was rewired about two years ago, so it is not falling down. But it is a fixer, priced like a fixer, and it has not sold in a month and a half at that price.

What it does do is put a number in a buyer's head before they walk into your house. That is the real reason presentation matters here. A clean, empty, well-photographed home down the street from an acknowledged fixer makes your home look like the easy choice. An occupied home with a tenant's furniture in the photos narrows that gap.

04 · What I left out
04Higher sales nearby

Why some bigger numbers are not your comps.

You will see higher sales close to you, and someone will eventually mention one. Here is what I looked at and set aside, and why.

  • 500 Silkwood Dr — closed $565,000. Westwood Hills HOA at $93 a month, a much smaller lot, and extensively updated inside. Buyers were paying for the finishes and the subdivision.
  • 13260 Erin Dr — closed $550,000. Fiddler Green HOA with a pool, a park and tennis courts. People pay for amenities. Your home has no HOA, which is an advantage in monthly cost but it also means no amenity premium.
  • 10747 Mount Vernon Rd — closed $620,000. A 1.9 acre ranchette with horse property. Completely different buyer looking for completely different things.
  • 1175 Sunny Creek Ct — closed $755,000. Built in 2021 in the Sunny Creek Estates HOA. A new-construction subdivision home is not the same product as a 1999 home on a sloped lot.

A sale is not a comp just because it is close by. The HOA, the lot, the age and the condition all have to line up, or the comparison misleads you into pricing where buyers will not follow.

05 · Where this points
05What the comps point to
$509,000 occupied · $535,000 vacant

Your home is newer than most of these comps, sits on a comparable lot, has a good 3 bed 2 bath layout, and carries no HOA. Those are real advantages.

But it is materially more dated inside than the homes that closed at $555,000 and $558,625. Those had quartz kitchens, remodeled baths and new flooring. Yours has 1990s oak cabinets and white tile counters. The sloped, terraced backyard with older retaining walls is another gap buyers will price in.

The clearest read is 1545 Wimbledon. Same size, same lot, clean but not remodeled. It listed at $549,000 and closed at $520,000. And 214 Alta shows what happens at the wrong number: remodeled, and it still took 106 days and closed at $500,000.

So the honest range is $500,000 to $545,000, and where you land inside it is mostly a presentation decision, not a market decision. Occupied puts you near the bottom. Vacant and clean puts you near the top.
